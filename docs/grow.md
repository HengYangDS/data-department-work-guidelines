# Grades, Tiers, and Growth

**When to use:** You set what a grade expects, review a member's work over a
period, decide a promotion, set a hiring bar, or coach a member toward the next
grade.

The department judges one piece of work, and a member's professional competence
and work performance over a period, separately, each by its own standard. A
[work score](evolve.md#use-scores-with-care), where scoring is used, judges the
delivery risk of one piece of work. A tier judges a member's work over one
period, at the member's own grade. A grade states the responsibility a member
has shown they can reliably carry. All three rest only on work the member did:
the professional understanding, capability, and quality of delivered work it
shows. Projects and other documents use these grades and tiers as written and do
not redefine them.

## Three Scales, One Line of Evidence

| Scale      | What it judges                                                  | Range         | When                                  |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Work score | One piece of work: its delivery risk                            | 1 to 5        | When the deliverable is reviewed      |
| Tier       | A member's work over one period, at their own grade             | S, A, B, C, D | Once per period                       |
| Grade      | The responsibility the member has shown they can reliably carry | Eight grades  | At hiring, then at a promotion review |

Evidence flows one way: work records, and work scores where used, support a tier;
tiers over several periods, with the responsibility actually carried, support a
change of grade. The entry grade is set at hiring against the grade's description.
You must not set a tier from one piece of work in place of the period's records,
or a grade from one period. A tier does not change a work score, and a grade does
not change a tier.

## The Professional Sequence

Every member is on one professional sequence. A grade is not a management
level: a team lead is a member of the sequence who also leads a team.

| Band         | Grade             | What the grade shows                                                                                                                           | Responsibility it can carry                                                                                  |
| ------------ | ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 器 tools     | 学徒 apprentice   | Learns the basic tools, the business context, and team norms under clear guidance.                                                             | Takes part in work; does not lead critical matters or state production, governance, or external conclusions. |
| 器 tools     | 专员 associate    | Under coaching, uses the tools and processes reliably by team norms and completes the steps of a given method.                                 | The steps of assigned work, whose result another person accepts.                                             |
| 术 craft     | 初级 junior       | Applies a method on their own to tasks with clear bounds and low risk, and states the method's basis, when it fails, and what was not checked. | Local implementation, organization, verification, and preparation, with explicit review.                     |
| 术 craft     | 中级 intermediate | Completes routine work independently and states its main risks and bounds.                                                                     | Lead responsibility for one module, one topic, or one low-risk data pipeline.                                |
| 法 method    | 高级 senior       | Leads complex problems, coordinates the adjacent owners, and gives evidence.                                                                   | Team lead; lead responsibility for a critical data pipeline, a complex analysis, or a platform module.       |
| 法 method    | 资深 staff        | Solves recurring structural problems across projects and roles, and turns the method into a standard, a tool, or a review rule.                | Team lead; direction, mechanism building, and critical review.                                               |
| 道 direction | 专家 expert       | Defines methodology, architecture routes, and the standard for judging hard problems.                                                          | Team lead or cross-team expert; routes, standards, and decisive vetoes.                                      |
| 道 direction | 首席 chief        | Sets department-level or cross-department technical direction, paradigms, and critical architecture judgments.                                 | Department-wide technical authority across teams; does not lead a team.                                      |

A grade's requirements are the items stated in the two columns of its row. A grade
shows the responsibility a member can carry when the authorized owner assigns it;
it does not itself grant authority ([two kinds of
authority](charter.md#two-kinds-of-authority)). Each band changes the kind of
responsibility: the tools band uses tools and norms correctly, the craft band gets
work done with a method, the method band owns problems and builds methods, and the
direction band defines direction and the standards of judgment. At every grade a
member grows along the [capability
path](evolve.md#grow-capability-through-real-work).

"Independently" means the next person can use what was delivered as it is, with
what was not checked stated; it does not mean speed or fluency with tools. At
every grade, work done with Agents counts as the member's when the member set
its boundary, checked what came back, and answers for it ([delegate a
boundary](human-agent.md#delegate-a-boundary-not-a-pile-of-context)). Years in a
grade are not a requirement. A member below senior does not lead a team; a senior,
staff, or expert member may lead one while still delivering first-line work under
their own name.

## Tiers: Performance at One's Own Grade

A tier describes a member's work in one period at their own grade. Each tier has
one definition across the department, read at the height the grade sets: an
apprentice's B and a staff member's B describe different absolute levels, and
tiers at different grades are not compared. B is the performance the grade
expects, C is the passing line, and D falls below it. Adjacent tiers differ in
kind, not in amount. D's conditions are read first; above D, each tier answers one
yes-or-no question. Within the member's grade: whether every requirement has an
accepted and used result (C), and whether each has one in which the key judgment
is the member's own (B). Toward the next grade, two questions asked of a period
that meets B, each also needing its users' record of value: whether the same item
that the next grade's "What the grade shows" adds shows in at least two different
pieces of work (A), and whether a responsibility that the next grade's
"Responsibility it can carry" adds was delegated to the member and carried for a
full cycle (S).

| Tier | 中文     | The member's work in the period, at their own grade                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ---- | -------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| S    | 远超预期 | Far exceeds expectation: meets B, and one responsibility that the next grade's "Responsibility it can carry" adds to the member's own row, other than leading a team while below senior, was formally delegated to the member, who carried it for one full cycle of that responsibility; its users record value at the scope that responsibility names.                            |
| A    | 超出预期 | Exceeds expectation: meets B, and the same item that the next grade's "What the grade shows" adds to the member's own row shows in at least two different pieces of work assigned at the member's grade; its users record what the result changed, at the scope of the piece of work it came from.                                                                                 |
| B    | 符合预期 | Meets expectation: meets C, and every requirement the period's assignments touched has at least one accepted and used result in which the key judgment was the member's own.                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| C    | 及格     | Passes: every requirement the period's assignments touched has at least one result that was accepted and used.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| D    | 不及格   | Below the passing line: at least one requirement the period's assignments touched has no accepted and used result in any of the period's work; or an agreed improvement period ended in the period with a requirement it named recorded as not met; or the work crossed the same [non-negotiable boundary](charter.md#four-non-negotiable-boundaries) twice or more in the period. |

When one of D's conditions holds, the period reads D whatever the other rows show;
otherwise it reads the highest tier its records show. A and S each require B; S
does not require A. S and A differ in kind and in the reach of their value, not in
the count of their evidence: A shows the next grade's capability in the member's
own work, with value at the scope of the piece of work it came from, while S means
one of the next grade's responsibilities was delegated and carried, with value at
that responsibility's scope. For an intermediate member, the responsibility S asks
for is lead responsibility for a critical data pipeline, a complex analysis, or a
platform module, carried for one full cycle; leading a team stays at senior and
above.

A requirement is each item of work or capability stated in the two columns of the
grade's row, such as an intermediate member's "states its main risks and bounds";
a limit such as "does not lead a team" is not one, and an item the period's
assignments did not touch does not count. A result counts as accepted and used
when it was accepted and taken into use, even if another person supplied or
corrected its key judgment, and a late result counts once it is accepted and used;
a returned result, or one due in the period and still undelivered at its end, does
not. Other than a result due in the period and still undelivered at its end, work
whose delivery, acceptance, or use falls after the period's end does not touch a
requirement in that period; it is read in the period in which it is used.
Judgments the grade's own row leaves to others, such as an apprentice's guidance,
an associate's acceptance, or a junior member's explicit review, count as the
member's own for B. What the member's own row already asks, a prediction included,
counts toward B only: an intermediate member who names the main risks of their
work before the result shows B, not A; a staff member who turns a method into a
review rule shows B, not A, and shows A only when the work shows what the expert
row's "What the grade shows" adds, such as setting the standard for judging hard
problems.

A full cycle is one complete round of the work that a responsibility governs, or
to which a method, standard, check, or judgment applies, from its start to
acceptance, such as one reporting period or one release. S is read in the period
in which the delegated responsibility's full cycle ends, or, when the result's
use falls after that period, in the period in which it is used; before that
cycle ends, the responsibility gives no period an S. Value is what the users of
a result record: who used it, and what it changed in research, production, a
decision, cost, or risk. Without that record, neither A nor S holds. The chief
grade has no next row. Before the period, the decision owner states in writing
what counts as A, a capability beyond the chief row shown in the member's own
work, and what counts as S, a responsibility beyond that row delegated to the
member and carried for a full cycle; for both, a user other than the decision
owner records the value. A tier not stated before the period cannot be read.

A single boundary crossing follows the charter's [breach
rule](charter.md#four-non-negotiable-boundaries) and does not by itself set the
tier. Two crossings of the same boundary in a period read D, because the
boundaries are the conditions under which others can rely on the member; one event
counts as one crossing, however many records show it. After a C, the member keeps
the grade, and the next period gives each requirement that had no result with the
member's own key judgment a goal and a coach, with an in-person check in the
middle of the period; when the same requirement falls short again in that next
period, the member and the decision owner agree on an improvement period. After D,
they agree on one at once. An improvement period is agreed in writing; it names
the requirements to be met and, for each, whether it is met by C's test or by B's,
the training or coaching given, and its length, which is one period unless they
agree otherwise, and at its end each named requirement is recorded as met or not
met by that test, with its records.

You should use a tier to show each member where they stand at their grade and
where to go next, to recognize and reward good work, to plan development, as
evidence for promotion, and in selection. Pay follows the grade and attainment,
meaning how far the member reached the goals agreed for the period; rewards and
sanctions take the tier as one of their grounds.

After the first two periods, the department reviews whether assessors agree at
each boundary between adjacent tiers, and rewrites the wording of a boundary where
they do not.

## Evidence for a Tier

Evidence is a record a second assessor can open: delegation and acceptance
records; delivery states and acceptance conclusions; review comments and the
member's replies; predictions recorded with their time before the result;
checks the member designed or maintains, with their trial runs; handover and
decision records; decisions others made relying on the member's conclusion;
judgments made independently by people the member coached; and records by a
result's users of who used it and what it changed.
Self-description, titles, demonstrations, tool lists, and facts or Agent output
the member did not check are leads, not evidence.

- A must rest on at least three independent pieces of evidence from at least two
  different pieces of work; a prediction recorded before the result and borne out
  by it is one kind of that evidence when what it shows is the item the next grade
  adds. S must rest on the delegation record, the record of the responsibility's
  full cycle, and its users' record of value. Another witness may add to them but
  not replace one.
- B, C, and D state, requirement by requirement, the records they rest on, and a D
  names each of its conditions that holds: the delivery records that show no
  accepted and used result, the written improvement agreement and its closing
  record, or the two boundary records.
- One strong piece of work does not raise a tier to A, and one weak piece does not
  lower a tier while another piece in the period meets the same requirement; S
  rests on its delegated responsibility, however many pieces of work that is.
- Evidence should be reproducible: a prediction is recorded and signed by the
  member before the result is known to the member or to any Agent working for
  them, a check has rejected a sample known to be wrong, a method is written so
  a second person can run it and knows when it fails, and a conclusion states
  its basis, scope, and what was not checked.

## Assess Fairly

The same grade and the same evidence must lead to the same tier. A tier is read
against the member's own grade, never against other people. You must not set a
quota or a forced distribution; a tier left empty in a period means no one
reached it then. The department head is the decision owner for tiers and
promotions, unless they designate a team lead for a team; the decision owner
chooses two assessors who know the work, neither of them the member.

1. **Declare first.** Before a period starts, you must publish its length,
   each grade's requirements, the tier definitions, the evidence rules, and this
   procedure.
2. **Judge separately.** Two assessors each judge in writing from the original
   records, not from summaries. When either assessor reads S, the decision owner
   adds an assessor from another domain, who judges in the same way; step 3 then
   compares the highest and the lowest of the three tiers.
3. **Reconcile on evidence.** When the assessors' tiers are adjacent, or are B and
   S and every assessor's written judgment answers A's question no, they re-read
   the evidence together; if they still differ, the decision owner chooses between
   the two from the records and states why. When their tiers differ in any other
   way, they record "insufficient evidence", name what to examine next, and judge
   again once it is examined. They do not average or use half tiers.
4. **Confirm and record.** The decision owner confirms the tier, which is
   recorded with its period, scope, and the records it rests on.
5. **Review on request.** The member may ask for one review with new evidence;
   the result and its reasons are recorded.

If the evidence still cannot settle the tier, the period carries no tier; it
neither counts toward nor breaks a run of periods for promotion.

A tier travels with its period, scope, and record, and you must not cite it
without them. Each period is assessed afresh. Only the member, the assessors,
and the decision owner see a tier; the decision owner carries it into the
reward, promotion, and selection decisions it informs. It is never published
with a name, placed in a file an Agent reads, or computed automatically. Each
period, you should publish the assessors' agreement rate and the share of
"insufficient evidence" as counts.

A tier is assessed and read against the grade requirements and tier definitions
declared for its period; a later revision of either does not reopen it.

## Promotion

A member may be promoted only when they have reached S or A at their grade in at
least two consecutive periods and already carry one of the next grade's
responsibilities, other than leading a team while below senior, in their actual
work, read from the same delegation records as S; entering senior or expert also
requires the evidence below. The count restarts after a grade change.

- **Entering senior:** in each of at least two different pieces of work, a
  method the member designed was adopted by another person, ran a full cycle,
  and held when conditions changed; the methods need not be the same. At least
  once, the member predicted, before the result was known, where a piece of
  their work was most likely to fail; the result bore the prediction out, and
  another person used the member's conclusion directly.
- **Entering expert:** a standard, check, or judgment the member set holds when
  the member is not there, has been adopted for a full cycle by people outside
  the member's own work, and has been found fit to use by a cross-domain review
  that stated when it stops applying.

Years, calendar time, and a single high score do not replace any of this
evidence.

After the first year of use, the department reviews how many consecutive periods
promotion requires.

## Hiring

A grade's description is its hiring bar. Interview evidence sets the entry
grade; you should read interview scores against the target grade, and they do
not convert into a tier. The first tier comes from the first period in post.
